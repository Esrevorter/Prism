## What is Prism, in one paragraph

Prism is digital money that keeps your finances **your business**. Every payment is private by default — nobody watching the network can see who paid whom, or how much. But unlike other private coins, Prism also lets you *choose* what to reveal: prove to your accountant that you reported all your income, or to a landlord that you have enough savings, **without exposing anything else**. And because self-custody has historically been scary and brittle, Prism's wallet is designed to be *forgiving* — set up in about a minute with a password and a 12-word recovery phrase, confirm every payment with a quick picture puzzle that proves *a human chose this*, and recover anytime without custodians. An on-device assistant turns *"Send $50 to Alex"* into an exact transaction and explains it in plain English before anything is signed. It was designed around the life of an independent creative professional — irregular project income from around the world, lots of small payouts to collaborators, zero appetite for financial surveillance — but those features are useful for everyone.

## The five promises (and how they actually work)

<table>
<tr><td width="50%">

### 🔒 Private by default
Every transaction hides sender, receiver, and amount — using the same class of battle-tested cryptographic techniques that make Monero private (ring signatures, stealth addresses, hidden amount commitments), plus network-level relay protection so your IP isn't leaked when you pay.

### 🌈 Transparent by choice — *The Prism Protocol*
Need to prove something? Generate a **selective disclosure**: a mathematical proof of exactly one fact ("my balance exceeds X", "these funds didn't come from a sanctioned address") for exactly one recipient, with an expiry date. Your full ledger stays hidden. We're honest about the semantics: you can *limit and expire* disclosures, but nothing on earth can recall a proof someone already accepted.

</td><td width="50%">

### 🛟 Forgiving custody, made simple
Your key lives on your machine, sealed by your password, protected by a 12-word recovery phrase that restores the *exact same address*. Lose the device? The phrase brings everything back — no custodian, no ceremony, no jargon. *(v0.1.x replaced the original multi-device MPC design with this lean model — see spec Amendment A2 and "How signing works" below.)*

### 🤖 Helpful, never pushy AI
An on-device assistant turns *"Send $50 to Alex for the session, keep it private"* into an exact transaction, shows you a plain-language preview, flags scams, and waits for **you** — a human picture-puzzle tap plus your password — before anything happens. The AI **never signs anything itself**, and your key never leaves the machine.

### 💸 Pennies, not surprises
Sending costs a fixed base fee of **0.0001 PRSM** (~$0.001) — no auction, no math anxiety. In-app payment channels make tips and micropayments effectively free and instant.

</td></tr>
</table>

### ✨ How signing works (the v0.1.x way)

```text
 ① You ask        "Pay Alex ≈ 12.4 PRSM ($50)"  → plain-language review card
 ② Human gate     a 3×3 picture puzzle bound to THIS transaction's fingerprint —
                  single-use, 2-minute expiry. Proves a person clicked; proves
                  nothing changed after you looked. (Honest scope: it is a
                  presence check, not identity proof.)
 ③ Key gate       your password unlocks the locally sealed spend key. Nothing
                  is ever transmitted — signing happens on your machine.
 ④ Receipt        animated confirmation, copyable disclosure ID, full history.
```

## Try it yourself (no coding needed)

Grab the installer for your OS from [Releases](https://github.com/Esrevorter/Prism/releases/latest) — **Windows** `.msi`, **macOS** `.dmg`, **Linux** `.deb`/`.AppImage` — run the ~60-second first-time wizard (password → write down 12 words → faucet demo), and send yourself a private test payment. Prefer the command line? Each release also carries a portable `prism` CLI zip:

```console
$ prism params                      # the money rules, straight from code
$ prism mine --blocks 3 --network refraction
$ prism gui                         # opens the wallet in your browser
```

Verify downloads against `SHA256SUMS`, and (since v0.1.5) every asset carries a keyless **cosign/sigstore signature** bound to this repo's GitHub Actions identity — instructions in [docs/ATTESTATION.md](docs/ATTESTATION.md).

Prefer pictures and step-by-step? Follow [Wiki → Getting Started as a User](https://github.com/Esrevorter/Prism/wiki/Getting-Started-as-a-User).
Prefer building from source? See [Wiki → Getting Started as a Developer](https://github.com/Esrevorter/Prism/wiki/Getting-Started-as-a-Developer) — pure Python 3.10+, **zero third-party dependencies**.

## Privacy: what we can honestly promise

We'd rather tell you the truth than oversell. Full list in [Wiki → FAQ](https://github.com/Esrevorter/Prism/wiki/FAQ); headline version:

✅ **We promise:** amounts, senders, and receivers hidden on-chain by default · no company ever holds your keys · your spend key and password never leave your machine · disclosures are scoped, expiring, and logged locally so you always know what you revealed · AI runs entirely on your hardware.

⚠️ **We don't promise:** perfect anonymity against a global adversary with unlimited resources (nobody can — even cash has limits) · recall of a proof you already handed over · protection against malware that watches your screen while you solve the puzzle (it proves presence, not identity — stated plainly in the UI).

## Frequently asked short answers

**Is Prism anonymous like Tor?** No — it's *private by default with user-controlled disclosure*, which is stronger for daily life and more honest about its limits.
**Who controls the network?** Miners and node operators. There is no company-held key, no admin switch, and no pre-mine.
**Can I recover a lost wallet?** Yes — your 12-word phrase restores the exact same address on a fresh install.
**Why a picture puzzle instead of a seed ceremony?** Because adoption dies at friction. The puzzle binds your approval to the exact bytes being signed and expires in two minutes; the password protects the key at rest. Both are explained in-context, in plain language, inside the app.
**Can a government see my balance?** Not on-chain. They can compel *you* to disclose — which is exactly why the disclosure tooling exists: you control scope, recipient, and expiry.
**Is there a token sale?** No. Zero pre-mine, no sale, ever. Funding comes from transparent future emission only.
